Understanding Section 140(1) ITA 1967

Section 140(1) empowers the Director General of Inland Revenue to disregard or vary certain transactions or arrangements involving tax avoidance or tax evasion. This provision helps prevent companies from using arrangements to obtain unintended tax advantages.

In Maju Mampan Sdn Bhd's Case Study 2, several items require careful tax review, including the EPF and staff training expenses (Note 3), interest on investment in shares (Note 5), professional and legal fees (Note 6), and other expenses reported in the financial statements.

💡 Key Learning Point

A tax adjustment does not automatically mean that tax avoidance has occurred. Each transaction must be evaluated based on its actual nature, commercial purpose, supporting documentation and applicable tax provisions.

Companies should practise responsible tax planning by maintaining transparency, complying with the Income Tax Act 1967 and avoiding artificial arrangements intended to obtain improper tax benefits.

The AaDab Ethical Index evaluates the ethical responsibility of the company's tax planning arrangements through four assessment dimensions.

Answer the following questions to calculate the company's Ethical Index Score. Each dimension carries a maximum of 25 marks.

Does the company provide honest disclosure and maintain accurate records of its tax planning arrangements?

Does the company's tax planning approach demonstrate fairness and avoid arrangements that shift an unfair tax burden to others?

Does the company comply with applicable tax laws and maintain proper supporting documentation?

Does the company demonstrate responsibility towards society through genuine contributions to public welfare?
